Figure
Lateral is the Lissajous figure: the X oscillator moves the point left–right, the Y oscillator up–down. The other three treat each oscillator as a rotor — its wave paired with the same wave a quarter period ahead, which for a sine is a point going round a circle — and add the two circles together. Con-current rotors turn the same way and draw looped rings; counter-current rotors turn opposite ways and draw |fx + fy|-petalled stars. Amplitude is each rotor's radius, and δ shifts X's rotor against Y's, which turns the figure (at 1:1 it opens a point into a circle). Rose draws r(θ) = cos(n/d · θ): X's frequency is n, Y's is d, so the ratio presets walk the classic n/d table. Every synthesis stage still shapes the waves: a square Y wave snaps the rose's angle to four directions, FM bends the petals. Torus knot and Sphere are solid: X winds round a torus the long way and Y through its hole, so p:q draws the (p, q) torus knot; or X sets longitude and Y latitude on a sphere (1:2 is the tennis-ball seam). Amplitude X sets their size, amplitude Y the tube thickness or how far the path swings towards the poles. Drag the canvas to orbit them; Tilt, Perspective and depth shading in the 3D section apply. Waves drops the figure and plots the two waves themselves across the whole view, X above Y, over one full period of the pair, with their sum normalised to full height underneath. Construction view and the 3D lift explain the lateral figure only and stand down in the rotary geometries; the audio is the same two oscillators in every geometry.

The two shapers from Ableton Wavetable's oscillator. Warp bends the cycle's own clock rather than its amplitude: at +100% the entire cycle is crammed into its opening and the rest of the period is a flat tail; at −100% it lies flat and then fires off at the very end. Both ends of the cycle are fixed points of the bend, so the wave stays periodic and continuous at any setting — on a square it reads as PWM, on a saw as a bent ramp. Fold drives the wave into a reflecting folder: past ±1 the signal turns back on itself instead of clipping, so each extra decibel of drive buys another lobe per cycle and the harmonics grow without the shape ever leaving the rails. Bias offsets the signal into the folder, so the two halves fold differently and the result loses its symmetry (and gains even harmonics).

The ear is far less sensitive at the bottom of its range than in the middle, so sweeping a ratio from 1 to 16 over a 100 Hz base walks from nearly inaudible to piercing without the Volume slider moving. Each oscillator is weighted by its own fundamental against the ISO 226 60-phon contour, which flattens that out: the two axes of a wide interval arrive at comparable loudness, and sweeping a frequency no longer sweeps the level with it. The curve only ever attenuates — the correction is normalised so nothing is ever boosted above unity — so switching it on cannot clip the output. It costs headroom instead, which Volume gets back. It weights the fundamental, not each harmonic, so it evens out the pitch of a note rather than the spectrum inside it.

A KEEP trail's cost is the ink it lays down, not the arithmetic: every copy is stroked twice (once to clear its own path, once to draw it), so a long figure at sixteen copies fills the frame many times over. The copies are therefore rasterised into their own smaller layer and scaled back up — half the linear size is a quarter of the work — while the live curve is drawn straight onto the canvas at full resolution and stays sharp. AUTO keeps the copies pixel-sharp while the frame rate allows it and steps them down only when frames actually run long, so you pay for detail only where it is affordable.
FADE is an afterglow: each frame's curve is kept and eaten away over the next few, at a rate set by Persistence. KEEP does not fade at all — it draws Count extra copies of the figure, each Spacing further back in phase, so the trail is a set of standing copies rather than a smear. The live curve erases whatever it crosses, so it always reads solid and the copies give way to it. Either way the trail applies to the figure alone; the construction view's strips, rulers and dots are redrawn clean every frame.

The animated figure reads as a wire object turning about a central axis because it is one. sin(fx·t + δ) expands to sin(fx·t)·cos δ + cos(fx·t)·sin δ — the x of the fixed curve (sin fx·t, sin fy·t, cos fx·t), wound on a vertical cylinder of radius ax, turned by δ about that cylinder's axis. The flat figure is its shadow, and animating δ is spinning it. PHASE LIFT stops discarding the third coordinate. With Tilt and Depth at zero the picture is unchanged to the pixel — that is the proof, not a bug. Raise Tilt to look at it from off-axis (or drag on the figure), Depth to add perspective, and Show the flat shadow to keep the original figure visible underneath. The identity is exact for sine only. Setting δ = 0 and then δ = π/2 pins both coordinates, and nothing but a sinusoid satisfies what is left: a phase shift moves harmonic n by n·δ, which no single rotation can do. With a triangle, saw or square base wave, or with FM, sync, fold, crush or feedback in play, the lifted object is a close relative of the animation rather than the same thing. The waveform filter is the exception — it is linear, so it commutes with the rotation exactly. While the lift is on, δ no longer moves the waveform, so Phase rotates FM/AM modulators has nothing to act on.

The frame is drawn through the same projection as the figure, so it is not a picture of a coordinate system laid over the curve — it is the space the curve is in. Every ring, plane and tick takes the rotation, the orbit tilt and the perspective the figure takes, which is why a tick at 0.5 sits exactly where the figure's own 0.5 sits and the equator stays under it as the object turns. Orbitals are the great circles of the three coordinate planes, each wearing the colour of the axis it is normal to, so the flat horizontal ring reads as the plane the y axis stands on. Angles traces the yaw and the orbit tilt as arcs by driving the rotation from zero up to its live value and following where an axis lands — the arc is the path the axis actually took, so it cannot disagree with the picture. Frame turns with the figure makes it the object's own frame; switch it off and the frame is fixed to the view instead, so the figure turns inside a coordinate system that stays put.

Construction view
STRAIGHT turns each generator a quarter turn, so its amplitude is measured along the axis it drives, and scrolls a one-cycle window that always ends at the tracer. The tracer's dot therefore holds a fixed depth and moves only sideways while the wave slides past, and the two lines to the moving point stay exactly horizontal and vertical — the classic construction. FULL LAP lays each wave along its strip and shows the whole lap at once, at the cost of diagonal projections. RINGS rolls each wave up into a ring by plotting it against itself a quarter cycle later — a pure sine becomes a circle, any other wave a distorted loop. The tracer wanders round each ring at its own frequency, and its horizontal (top) or vertical (left) reading is the coordinate it feeds the figure.

Streams the two axis waveforms out as continuous controllers, so the same shapes that draw the figure can modulate something in a DAW. The rate is the visual one, not the audible one: Rate sets how many laps of the figure go by per second, and each axis then runs at its own side of the ratio — a 3:2 figure at 1 lap/s sends 3 Hz on X and 2 Hz on Y. The values carry the whole waveform chain, filter included, and are sent only when the 7-bit value actually changes.

Wavetable Export
C0 · 33.5 kHz
Writes one cycle of each axis's processed waveform as a 2048-sample WAV — the format wavetable synths and samplers expect. A single cycle has no pitch of its own; what fixes it is the file's sample rate, so the Root note sets that rate (2048 × the note's frequency) and the file plays back at that pitch at normal speed. C0 is the usual convention for single-cycle libraries; pick the note your sampler expects, or the one you want the table to sound at untransposed.
